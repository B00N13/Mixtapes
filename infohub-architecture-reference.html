00 · Orientation

Start here: what InfoHub was

InfoHub was a multi-site SharePoint Online information platform built around a deliberate separation of security/access, content ownership, and publication approval. The operating model also extended beyond initial publication: scheduled review tracking identified stale pages and prompted accountable owners to act.

Read this before sharing externally

These materials originate from an earlier employer’s internal documentation. This web edition preserves the architecture and operating model but omits private tenant URLs, operational identities and live links. Check your right to share it before publishing in a public GitHub repository or distributing it externally. The design is historical and is not a statement of Westport’s architecture.

The principles in one sentence

Security controls who can act. Ownership identifies who is accountable. Approval determines what may be published.

The platform’s original documentation described the model as aligned with ISO/IEC 27001 principles. Alignment was a design objective; these documents alone do not establish independent certification or audit approval.

How to use this guide

Start with the architecture map and permission tiers, then the two lifecycles. The later chapters retain more detailed build steps and technical notes, so an architect, administrator or content owner can each find their own entry point.

InfoHub was built as a SharePoint Hub with associated communication sites, a central ownership and tracking layer, and reusable Power Automate processing. Each site held content, while central lists and flows coordinated governance across sites.

Board isolation

Board content was explicitly not to be copied into ordinary InfoHub sites. The Board had its own secured site, with Tier 5 access and mandatory approval. The Hub could display a Board navigation link using audience targeting, but navigation visibility was not a security boundary; SharePoint permissions remained authoritative.

02 · Governance

Governance principles and controls

InfoHub’s governance objective was to keep published business information reliable, usable and auditable across multiple sites, without making everyday content management unnecessarily complicated.

LayerControlled byRule
SecurityEntra groups / SharePoint permission assignmentsNo direct permissions for individuals; role-based access at scale
OwnershipPage Owners grouping → named peopleEach content area has identifiable people who are accountable
ApprovalSharePoint moderation and Power AutomateContent is published only when the applicable approval route is satisfied
ReviewScheduled monitoring, owner reminders and a central registerPublished pages must remain current after release

Designed-in safeguards

  • IT/Governance retained the high-risk Full Control and configuration permissions.
  • Contributors could create and edit drafts without necessarily being able to approve or publish governed items.
  • Tier 1 approval rights were distinct from ordinary site administration rights.
  • Tier 4 approval rights applied only within appropriate restricted containers, not to unrelated public/general content.
  • Central approval records captured owners, source links, status and timestamps.
  • Published content was checked for currency using configurable review thresholds, separately from approval.
  • Users consumed content through pages organised by subject rather than needing to understand backend library boundaries.

Not interchangeable

Approval does not grant access, and access does not grant approval. A user with broad read or edit rights should not inherit approval rights merely because they can open the site.

03 · Security

Security model: T0–T5

Microsoft Entra (then commonly called Azure AD) groups were the single source of truth for broad access. Their membership was used through SharePoint groups and scoped folder permissions. The intent was never to assign one-off permissions directly to individual users.

TierRole and capabilityRepresentative group pattern
T0IT / governance administration: platform configuration, permissions and full controlSG_InfoHub_T0_Governance
T1Content Owners: approve and publish general controlled content; also accountable for contentSG_InfoHub_T1_<Domain>_Owners
T2Contributors: create, edit and submit content for approvalSG_InfoHub_T2_<Domain>_Contributors
T3General staff: read approved, published contentSG_InfoHub_T3_AllStaff
T4Confidential users: access and approve restricted content within specified containers onlySG_InfoHub_T4_<Domain>_Restricted
T5Board: dedicated secured site and Board-level content approvalSG_InfoHub_T5_Board

Domains

The original tier definitions listed Finance, HR, Infrastructure, Management, Marketing, Services, IT and Doctors as functional domains. These were broad access groupings, not the granular ownership categories assigned to individual pages.

Group-to-permission mapping

SharePoint security containerGroup / roleIntended permission
OwnersTier 0 GovernanceFull Control / platform administration
MembersTier 2 domain ContributorsEdit/create working content
VisitorsTier 3 All StaffRead published content
<Domain> ApproversTier 1 domain OwnersCustom permission level with Edit + Approve Items; explicitly without Full Control
Restricted folder permissionsTier 4 domain RestrictedRestricted access; Edit + Approve Items only where the restricted approval scope requires it
Board siteTier 5 BoardSeparate high-sensitivity access and approval

Restricted content boundaries

Restricted folders lived inside Site Pages, Documents and Controlled Documents. Their permission inheritance was broken at the designated parent folder, general Members/Visitors access was removed, and Tier 4 permissions were granted. Subfolders were required to inherit from that parent rather than create fragmented permission exceptions.

Access versus page ownership

Tier 1 domain access is a broad permission category. A named Page Owner (or multiple named owners) records responsibility for a particular content area. That narrower accountability is not represented by group membership alone.

04 · Ownership

Page Owners and content accountability

Ownership was maintained at a finer level than site/domain access. A single Services domain might have distinct CSO – Local, CSO – Remote and CSO – Specialist owners. Other examples documented included HR and IT Application Management.

FeatureHistorical design
GroupingA subject area or content category, not a broad security role
Owner recordsNamed person(s) responsible; the original site setup specified a multi-person Owner field
Content metadataSite Pages included a Page Owner lookup into the Page Owners list
Approval routingFlows resolved the Page Owner grouping to names/emails and appropriate approval handling
Review remindersPage Owner details were also used for overdue page tracking and notifications
Central coordinationTechnical flow documentation describes a central Page Owners register plus local site lists / synchronisation

Why not simply use security groups as owners?

A domain group can contain many people, and its membership may change. Treating that whole group as the content owner would make responsibilities and approval routing ambiguous. The named Page Owners model ensured that someone could actually be asked to check a page, coordinate a subject-matter expert, and sign off a change.

Multiple named owners could be associated with a content area. Delegating a review to an SME transferred the work of updating, not the final accountability for accuracy and approval.

Documentation nuance

The sources describe both a Page Owners list created at each new site and a central Page Owners source synchronised into local lists. The reconstructed guide retains both roles; the exact production synchronisation configuration would need to be inspected in the original tenant.

05 · Content architecture

Sites, libraries, page metadata and UX

The content structure separated ordinary documents from controlled documents, because SharePoint document approval is configured at library level. The end-user interface deliberately hid this backend split.

LocationContentApproval behaviour
Documents libraryGeneral documentsNo formal approval by default
Controlled Documents libraryApproval-controlled materialRequire content approval; major/minor versions
Site Pages libraryPages with metadata and page-owner assignmentLibrary approval configured; AutoApproval distinguishes selected pages / auto-approved routing
Restricted containersSensitive pages or documents within the above librariesSeparate Tier 4 permission boundary; approval where the content class requires it
Board siteAll Board materialsSeparate Tier 5 secure site; mandatory approval

Site Pages columns in the standard setup

ColumnType / purpose
A-Z Link DisplayYes/No; A–Z navigation display
InfoHubAZYes/No; navigation/indexing support
Orange CategoryHyperlink or Picture; category/breadcrumb integration
Page OwnerLookup into Page Owners list; resolves accountability and routing

Document and page presentation

Documents were grouped by subject (for example HR or Finance) within their respective libraries. A staff-facing SharePoint page could surface related items together across libraries, so readers saw subject-oriented content rather than a mixture of underlying storage rules. The original architecture explicitly left the final cross-library aggregation approach open: standard web parts, search-driven aggregation or SPFx.

Why check-in/check-out was excluded

The documents rejected check-in/check-out as a governance mechanism because it can confuse saved drafts, publication and approval; introduce stranded checkouts; and create misleading assumptions about whether something has been reviewed. Version history + approval and deliberate Submit for Approval were the intended mechanisms.

For Orange Category, breadcrumbs, A–Z and the four custom SPFx apps: see Navigation, metadata and the staff experience.

06 · Navigation & user experience

Navigation, metadata and the staff experience

InfoHub was more than a collection of SharePoint libraries. It combined centrally consistent site navigation, page-level metadata and custom SPFx components so that staff could discover information through familiar pathways while the underlying sites, security and publishing rules remained separate.

Source-backed structureCustom SPFx layerCode-level behaviour to verify

What the original documents explicitly establish

The standard site-build procedure required an InfoHub Hub association, an inherited theme, a Compact header, Cascading navigation, no footer, four named SPFx apps, and Site Pages fields for A–Z, Orange Category and Page Owner. It expressly describes these columns as supporting navigation (breadcrumbs), categorisation and ownership tracking. The precise runtime algorithms and visual appearance of those apps are not described in the PDFs.

The standard build process began by creating a SharePoint Communication Site from the Human Resources starter template, associating it with the InfoHub Hub, and applying a common presentation. The theme and Hub navigation were inherited. Each associated site used a Compact header, Cascading navigation style and disabled footer.

Permission-trimming check for any future rebuild

Any aggregated A–Z listing should be tested to ensure users can only discover and follow links they are authorised to access. That is an architectural validation requirement, not an assertion that the original A–Z SPFx app implemented a particular filtering technique.

5. On-page Table of Contents and reading aids

Two additional SPFx applications were part of the standard installation:

OccuMED Table of Contents

Intended to support navigation within structured pages. The setup guide establishes that it was installed, but does not define its exact heading selection, rendering, anchor or accessibility logic.

OccuMED List Width Toggle

A page/list presentation enhancement. The guide establishes it as a required install, but does not identify specific target lists, breakpoint behaviour or the persistence of a user's width preference.

The wider design principle was that navigating a long page and finding the right page are different user needs. Hub navigation, breadcrumbs, A–Z discovery and an on-page Table of Contents address different parts of that journey.

6. Search, content discovery and navigation boundaries

SharePoint provides search and site navigation features, but the uploaded InfoHub PDFs do not describe a separate InfoHub search component, custom search refiners, a particular search index design, or a complete search-results page. Avoid assuming an advanced search implementation merely because InfoHub had an A–Z feature.

The original architecture also notes that the final way of presenting documents from multiple libraries together was still to be confirmed, with standard web parts, search-based aggregation or custom SPFx under consideration. Do not present cross-library aggregation as a completed feature solely from that design note.

7. Authoring and maintaining the navigation experience

The standard configuration suggests the following editorial checklist for a Site Page. It combines documented field requirements with sensible validation questions; it is not a claim that InfoHub had a single automated page-creation wizard.

01 — Choose the correct domain site. Organise content under the relevant associated Communication Site and ensure the user has suitable authoring permissions.
02 — Create or update the SharePoint page. Use the site's common branding, navigation and page layout conventions.
03 — Set the Page Owner. Choose the appropriate functional entry from the Page Owners lookup; this is the source of accountability and approval routing.
04 — Review navigation metadata. Check Orange Category, InfoHubAZ and A-Z Link Display against the actual component rules before publishing.
05 — Validate the staff view. Test Hub navigation, breadcrumb links, expected A–Z visibility and page headings/TOC at desktop and mobile widths.
06 — Publish using the governed process. Follow the existing Site Pages approval and versioning rules, rather than treating visible navigation links as a substitute for publication approval.
07 — Review after changes. After renaming, moving or retiring a page, check for orphaned navigation links and update review/owner details as required.

The AutoApproval nuance

The source documents specify two automation cues: an item/page AutoApproval setting, or a matching requestor and authorised owner email. This supports self-service approval by an eligible owner while still directing a contributor’s submitted change for review. 08 · Reviews

Page review lifecycle: keeping content current

The Content Review Tracking System was distinct from the initial approval workflow. Its objective was to find published pages that had not been reviewed recently enough, notify accountable owners and maintain a central register until those pages were brought current.

Weekly scheduled scan
→
Enabled rules from
InfoHub Page Review Config
→
Compare page date vs threshold
→
Update InfoHub Overdue Pages
→
Daily grouped owner notifications

How it worked

  • The weekly flow traversed the published Site Pages across the applicable InfoHub sites.
  • Each site had a configured review threshold, measured in months, in InfoHub Page Review Config.
  • Last modified date was compared with that threshold; overdue published pages generated or updated records in InfoHub Overdue Pages.
  • The register retained page identity, source link, owners, overdue status and FirstSeen/LastSeen timestamps.
  • A daily notification process read the register and sent consolidated notifications to owners, rather than one disconnected message per page.
  • When pages were updated and approved/published as current, their overdue records were resolved or removed from active tracking.
  • Drafts were excluded from review checks; pages lacking resolvable owner email metadata were excluded from notification processing.

Why two separate workflows?

Before publishingAfter publishing
Approval workflowScheduled review workflow
Is this change authorised to be seen by readers?Is this existing published information still current?
Queue and decision tracked in InfoHub ApprovalsOverdue state tracked in InfoHub Overdue Pages
Triggered around relevant content changes / moderation statesWeekly scans and daily notification processing

Important user instruction

A content owner could not clear overdue status by simply opening the page. The documented process required editing the page (even if no substantive changes were necessary), submitting it for approval and completing the publication workflow to refresh its recorded currency.

Idempotency

The approval key combined site + library + full content/folder path, preventing two files of the same name in different folders from being conflated. Flows checked for an existing record and updated it rather than repeatedly creating duplicates. Overdue tracking used the same general single-record principle.

Why copy owner details into tracking records?

Notification flows could work directly from the central queue without repeatedly looking up owner information. The record also retained a useful ownership snapshot for tracing historical actions, even as live ownership assignments evolved.

11 · Technical reference

Technical deep dive: approval processing

Site-level Queue Sync

Queue Sync flows listened to Site Pages and Controlled Documents changes. For each eligible event, the workflow evaluated source moderation state, Page Owner, library context, content path, requestor, existing register match and auto-approval rules.

Owner resolution and stable keys

The source item’s Page Owner lookup identified the responsible functional grouping. The flow resolved names and email addresses (including multiple owners), then stored them with the approval record. The Approval Key used the source site, library and folder-aware path to locate the correct record on subsequent runs.

Auto-approval

The documented design allowed automatic approval if an item was marked for auto-approval or the requestor matched an authorised owner email. Otherwise a Pending item entered the central owner-notification route.

Duplicate notification prevention

The NotificationSent field prevented repeat owner emails for an existing request. Requestor-completion notifications used separate tracking so their state was not confused with the owner email state.

Outcome sync

Following a decision, the processing flow maintained approval status, source link, requestor/owner details, completion details and notification state. Approval completion updated the relevant source content (publication/rejection) and its central register entry.

What is not contained in the source documents

The PDFs describe processing logic and list fields, but do not include exportable flow definitions, complete Power Automate expressions, exact trigger URLs or executable source code. This is a design reference, not a runnable migration package.

Review State Sync

A scheduled flow loaded enabled rows from InfoHub Page Review Config, cached Page Owners, scanned the relevant Site Pages libraries and evaluated published page dates against site-specific timeframes. It created/updated overdue rows and marked resolved rows when content became current.

Owner reminder batching

The Overdue Page Notifications process read the central list, grouped overdue pages by owner and sent each owner a single consolidated email covering their outstanding content. Records without an owner email were not included.

Resolution and retention

Once updated and published, a page that fell within the review threshold was marked resolved. The technical design allowed resolved records to be retained temporarily before cleanup for short-term traceability.

Practical governance implication

Missing Page Owner metadata is a data quality and governance issue, not just an email error: a page could fail to generate an actionable reminder if its ownership was not recorded correctly.

New InfoHub site: implementation checklist

The original New InfoHub Site — Standard Setup Guide prescribed the same foundation for each subject-domain communication site. This checklist restates the actual setup sequence so an administrator can see how the model was operationalised.

01 — Create communication site. Start with the Human Resources communication-site template. Use naming pattern /sites/<Domain>Info for the site URL.
02 — Associate with InfoHub Hub. Confirm shared Hub navigation and automatically inherited theme.
03 — Apply the standard presentation. Compact header, Cascading navigation, Footer disabled. Theme inherited from Hub.
04 — Install SPFx components. OccuMED A–Z Page, List Width Toggle, Orange Category Integration and Table of Contents.
05 — Create Page Owners. Default Title for functional grouping; Owner Person/Group column allowing multiple owners.
06 — Configure Site Pages columns. A-Z Link Display, InfoHubAZ, Orange Category, Page Owner lookup.
07 — Create Controlled Documents. Keep ordinary Documents separate from the formally approved library.
08 — Configure moderation. Require content approval on Site Pages and Controlled Documents; major/minor versioning; check-in/check-out disabled. Authors use Submit for Approval.
09 — Configure security. Retain Owners / Members / Visitors. Map Tier 0 → Owners, Tier 2 → Members, Tier 3 → Visitors. Create <Domain> Approvers SharePoint group, custom Edit + Approve Items (no Full Control), assign Tier 1 domain Owners. Remove directly assigned individual users.
10 — Configure restricted folders. Create the standard restricted containers; break permissions only at those parent folders; remove broad Members / Visitors; apply Tier 4; keep child subfolders inheriting.

14 · User procedure

For Page Owners: approval notification

A Page Owner could receive an approval email after submitting their own change, after a Contributor/SME submitted a change, or after an overdue page was reviewed and resubmitted for controlled publication.

Open the item. Use the email’s Open item link; use the Library link if the SharePoint approval controls are needed.
Review content. Check accuracy, currency, business relevance, completeness, appropriateness and any delegated review.
Approve or reject. Approve only when suitable for publication; reject when changes, missing information or further review are required.
Communicate as necessary. If a contributor submitted the change, explain rejection reasons and request updates/resubmission.
DecisionExpected outcome
ApprovedContent published; status recorded; associated review requirement cleared where applicable
RejectedContent stays unpublished; author can revise and resubmit

Delegating review or editing did not transfer final accountability. The named Page Owner remained responsible for accuracy and timely approval.

15 · User procedure

For Page Owners: overdue review notification

The overdue email identified published content that had passed its review threshold. Its owner could complete the review personally or delegate the editing to an SME.

Open the page. Use the link in the notification.
Review or delegate. Check relevance, policy changes, outdated references, document links and usability; optionally ask an SME to update.
Save updates. Edit the page as needed, even if the review establishes that the original information is still correct.
Submit for Approval. Saving a page is not sufficient to complete the review lifecycle. Submission refreshes publication and currency tracking.
Confirm approval outcome. If an authorised Page Owner submits, auto-approval may apply; if a delegate submits, route back to the owner for decision.
Confirm resolution. Approved/published content is reassessed and the overdue status is cleared from active tracking.

Critical operational detail

The historical user guide explicitly required editing and submitting for approval even when no content change was necessary, so the recorded published date could be refreshed and the overdue flag cleared. The workflow must not be reduced to a simple “I have read it” acknowledgement.

Notifications might recur while overdue, and multiple owners might receive the same notice. Accountability stayed with the Page Owner even when an SME carried out the update.

Service account connectionsConsistent identity for SharePoint / Microsoft 365 workflow operations

Service account dependencies

Service account access had to remain valid for source InfoHub sites, Site Pages, Controlled Documents, central lists and any mailbox used by notification flows. If permissions or connection credentials were removed, a flow might stop triggering or fail during reads, writes or email actions.

High-value opening questions

“Which types of information should be controlled before publication, and who is accountable for keeping each subject area current?”

Establishes governance requirements before choosing library settings or automation.

“If the main content owner is away for a month, how do approvals and overdue reviews keep moving?”

Tests business continuity and whether named ownership requires deputies / escalation.

“What security model and SharePoint architecture are already in place, and what should any new information hub integrate with?”

Avoids building a parallel permission system without understanding the existing platform.

Adapt rather than copy

A future Westport implementation should be designed against Westport’s information classification, retention, identity governance, approval policy and operational support model. Reusing the InfoHub structure unchanged could create security or maintenance problems.
